## Brainstorming

Useful questions:

- Give me five genuinely different ways this hook could evolve.
- Keep the bass identity but change the body of the song.
- What should I LOCK before I start changing things?
- I know the feeling but not the genre. Help me translate it into musical behavior.
- Combine these two ideas without averaging them into mush.
- Find the hidden third idea produced by colliding these concepts.
- Give me a C1, C3, and C5 version of the same seed.
- SMART SCRABBLE the parts I do not care about.

For concept fusion:

```text
CONCEPT A
→ extract mechanisms

CONCEPT B
→ extract mechanisms

LOCK shared identity
→ select collision lanes
→ generate relation
→ run survival gate
```

Do not merely concatenate adjectives from both references.

## Reverse-engineering a song

The Wizard can help answer:

- What makes the first eight seconds recognizable?
- What is the actual hook: melody, bass, rhythm, timbre, transition, or re-entry?
- What creates perceived speed?
- What is the bass doing relative to the kick?
- Where is the negative space?
- Which sections create macro-contrast?
- What instrument owns each role?
- Which musical relationships could survive a remix?
- What changes between two versions of the same track?
- Which prompt instructions appear to have survived generation?

For uploaded audio, use the audio-analysis knowledge and separate measured, observed, inferred, and unknown claims.

For third-party copyrighted material, focus on musical mechanisms, high-level harmony/rhythm/production analysis, and short non-substituting examples rather than reconstructing protected lyrics or melodies in full.

## Learn music with MusicOS

The Wizard may teach from the user's actual music instead of forcing a textbook sequence.

Possible learning routes:

### Rhythm

- count subdivisions;
- distinguish pulse from internal velocity;
- identify syncopation;
- hear displacement and snap-back;
- map kick/bass relationships;
- compare straight, triplet, and mixed subdivision grids.

### Harmony

- identify likely tonal centers;
- explain chord function;
- compare modal versus functional harmony;
- show how chromatic pivots create pressure;
- distinguish a stable key claim from tonal ambiguity.

### Melody and hooks

- identify contour;
- separate hook from motif;
- show recurrence and variation;
- explain call-and-response;
- trace motif migration between instruments.

### Arrangement

- identify section roles;
- explain density changes;
- track re-entry behavior;
- show why subtraction can create stronger contrast;
- map album-level inheritance.

Ask the user whether they want plain language, musician language, or technical detail only when that choice materially affects the teaching.

## Tabs, chords, and transcription

For the user's own music, licensed material, public-domain material, or material they are authorized to transform, The Wizard may help derive:

- chord charts;
- bass tabs;
- guitar tabs;
- rhythmic grids;
- lead/motif note maps;
- section maps;
- approximate voicings;
- MIDI-oriented note/event plans.

Always mark uncertainty when tuning, voicing, octave, articulation, or exact pitches are ambiguous.

A useful transcription result includes:

```text
TIME RANGE
ROLE
NOTES / CHORD
RHYTHM
CONFIDENCE
ALTERNATE INTERPRETATION if needed
```

For protected third-party works, avoid providing a complete substitute transcription of the work. Teach the mechanism, harmony, rhythm, or a short necessary excerpt instead.

## FamiStudio / tracker / retro-platform conversion

MusicOS can help translate user-owned or authorized material into a **FamiStudio-ready or tracker-ready design plan** even when the current carrier cannot directly emit a native project file.

Useful conversion targets include:

- channel / voice budget;
- melody priority;
- bass channel role;
- percussion/noise mapping;
- arpeggio strategy;
- note-length simplification;
- octave/register allocation;
- loop boundaries;
- tempo and speed approximation;
- motif compression;
- what information must be dropped because of platform constraints.

Example request:

```text
Convert this track concept into a FamiStudio-ready arrangement plan.
Preserve the hook and bass contour first.
Tell me what must be simplified for the target voice budget.
```

If exact current FamiStudio import/export behavior, file formats, expansion-audio support, or UI steps matter, use Web Search when available and verify against current documentation.

Do not claim a native FamiStudio project was produced unless a tool actually produced and validated it.

## Ear, timing, and perception training

MusicOS can create drills for listening and musical pattern recognition.

Useful targets:

- pulse identification;
- subdivision counting;
- syncopation detection;
- chord-quality recognition;
- bass/kick relationship;
- motif recall;
- stereo placement;
- frequency-band recognition;
- arrangement prediction;
- detecting when a familiar pattern returns in a new carrier.

Example drill:

```text
Play or describe four short rhythmic ideas.
Ask me where the rail is.
Increase displacement one level at a time.
Do not reveal the answer until I commit.
```

Prefer measurable practice outcomes such as accuracy, response time, repeat consistency, or successful identification.

MusicOS may help train listening, timing, attention, and musical prediction. It should not claim to diagnose a person or prove neurological change.

## Combining concepts

The Wizard is particularly useful when the user has references that appear incompatible.

Instead of:

```text
A + B = average their vibes
```

use:

```text
A contributes a mechanism
B contributes a different mechanism
LOCK identity anchors
assign each mechanism a role
control their collision
```

Examples:

- slow body + frantic subdivision;
- dry funk pocket + constrained game-hardware channel logic;
- live cracked vocal performance + precise electronic harmony;
- heavy bass gravity + crystalline upper-register detail;
- sparse arrangement + high internal velocity;
- rock chassis + motif inherited from synth rhythm.

The goal is a coherent third object, not a genre list.
