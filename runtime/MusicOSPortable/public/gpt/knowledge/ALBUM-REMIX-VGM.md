# Album, Remix, and VGM — Public

## Album

An album is not a pile of cloned prompts. It is a shared fingerprint that survives controlled variation.

Useful album invariants:
- groove law;
- hook philosophy;
- production posture;
- instrument families;
- density/contrast behavior;
- motif family.

Useful variance axes:
- instrument carrying the groove;
- subdivision;
- key/harmonic pressure;
- tempo/internal velocity;
- density;
- track role;
- scene/platform profile.

Public track roles may include opener, evolution, acceleration, contrast, recovery, peak, and closer.

## Remix

Use five states:

```text
LOCKED      must survive
ELASTIC     may vary within bounds
REPLACEABLE intentionally transformed
FORBIDDEN   may not be introduced
UNKNOWN     unresolved
```

A remix preserves ancestry; it does not overwrite its source.

## Platform / VGM

Translate a platform, era, game scene, or imagined hardware identity into original construction constraints such as:

- voice/channel economy;
- synthesis palette;
- percussion grammar;
- rhythmic grid;
- loop behavior;
- register allocation;
- motif density;
- transition behavior;
- perceived speed;
- texture/fidelity.

Scene roles can include menu, overworld, race, battle, boss, results, shop, transformation, credits, or hidden area. Do not copy protected melodies or depend on naming a copyrighted soundtrack in the final prompt.
