```text

EINSTEIN
precise mechanism, units, ratios, structure, or signal behavior

BRAIN / BODY
what perception, auditory-motor timing, attention, arousal, prediction, or physiology plausibly contributes
```

# 3. Rhythm and time

## BEAT / PULSE

**ELI5:** the invisible footsteps the song walks on.  
**DRUMMER:** what your foot keeps tapping even during a fill.  
**EINSTEIN:** a periodic or inferred temporal reference used to organize events; listeners can perceive beat even when not every beat contains a sound.  
**BRAIN/BODY:** beat perception engages auditory and motor timing systems; the body often predicts the next beat before it arrives.  
**USE IT:** remove instruments and check whether the listener could still tap the same pulse.

## TEMPO

**ELI5:** how fast the footsteps are.  
**DRUMMER:** how fast the count-in moves.  
**EINSTEIN:** rate of the reference beat, commonly expressed in beats per minute.  
**USE IT:** separate tempo from perceived speed; subdivisions can make a slow tempo feel fast.

## BPM

**ELI5:** how many beat-footsteps happen in one minute.  
**EINSTEIN:** `BPM = beats / minute`; 120 BPM means two reference beats per second.  
**DON'T CONFUSE:** BPM with event density.

## RHYTHM

**ELI5:** the pattern of long, short, hit, and empty moments.  
**DRUMMER:** what your limbs actually play around the pulse.  
**EINSTEIN:** organization of event onsets, durations, accents, and silences through time.

## METER

**ELI5:** how the footsteps get grouped into repeating boxes.  
**DRUMMER:** which beats feel like `ONE` and which feel like the rest of the bar.  
**EINSTEIN:** hierarchical organization of beats into recurring strong/weak accent structures.

## TIME SIGNATURE

**ELI5:** the label on the beat-box.  
**DRUMMER:** `4/4` usually means count four quarter-note beats before the bar resets.  
**EINSTEIN:** notation describing beat grouping and nominal beat unit; it does not fully describe groove.

## DOWNBEAT

**ELI5:** the floor tile marked `ONE`.  
**DRUMMER:** the start of the bar.  
**USE IT:** hide it briefly, then make its return obvious for snap-back.

## OFFBEAT

**ELI5:** the spaces between the main footsteps.  
**DRUMMER:** often the `ands` in `1-and-2-and`.  
**USE IT:** place guitar or hats there for lift without changing tempo.

## SUBDIVISION

**ELI5:** cutting each beat into smaller pizza slices.  
**DRUMMER:** eighths, sixteenths, triplets, etc. under the same foot pulse.  
**EINSTEIN:** partition of an inter-beat interval into smaller temporal units.  
**BRAIN/BODY:** subdivision changes prediction density and perceived velocity even when beat rate is unchanged.

## SYNCOPATION

**ELI5:** putting the surprise where the listener expected empty space.  
**DRUMMER:** accent the `and`, delay the hit, or skip an expected strong beat.  
**EINSTEIN:** accent/onset structure that conflicts with or displaces metrical expectation.  
**BRAIN/BODY:** useful syncopation plays with prediction; too little is obvious, too much can erase the reference grid.  
**USE IT:** displace an accent, not necessarily the whole groove.

## TUPLET

**ELI5:** fitting a strange number of equal slices into space normally used by another number.  
**DRUMMER:** three notes where two usually fit, five where four usually fit, etc.  
**EINSTEIN:** division of a duration into `n` equal parts against the prevailing notated subdivision.  
**USE IT:** treat tuplets as color that returns to the rail.

## TRIPLET

**ELI5:** three equal hops inside one beat-space.  
**DRUMMER:** `trip-o-let`.  
**EINSTEIN:** 3-part equal subdivision, often contrasted with binary division.

## QUINTUPLET

**ELI5:** five tiny hops squeezed evenly into the same box.  
**DRUMMER:** count five evenly without speeding the beat itself.  
**USE IT:** one short quintuplet fill can sound alien while the bar stays grounded.

## POLYRHYTHM

**ELI5:** two repeating counting shapes sharing the same room.  
**DRUMMER:** one limb can imply 3 while another implies 2.  
**EINSTEIN:** simultaneous periodic rhythmic organizations with differing grouping ratios such as 3:2.  
**DON'T CONFUSE:** with random rhythmic complexity.

## GROOVE

**ELI5:** the reason the beat makes your body want to move.  
**DRUMMER:** the relationship between kick, snare, bass, hats, timing, accents, and space.  
**EINSTEIN:** perceived movement arising from timing, accent, pattern, microtiming, repetition, expectation, and interaction; not reducible to BPM.  
**BRAIN/BODY:** groove strongly couples auditory expectation with motor readiness and movement desire.

## POCKET

**ELI5:** where the band feels comfortably locked together.  
**DRUMMER:** the kick, snare, bass, and microtiming agree on where the groove lives.  
**USE IT:** change ornaments while protecting the pocket.

## MICROTIMING

**ELI5:** tiny early/late nudges that do not change the big clock.  
**DRUMMER:** laying a snare a little behind or pushing a hat slightly ahead.  
**EINSTEIN:** small onset deviations relative to a quantized or inferred grid.  
**DON'T CONFUSE:** with sloppy timing; intentional microtiming remains relational.

## SWING

**ELI5:** pairs of notes stop walking evenly and start limping in a good way.  
**DRUMMER:** long-short subdivision feel instead of equal straight eighths.  
**EINSTEIN:** systematic unequal timing of nominally equal subdivisions; ratio varies by tempo/style/performance.

## BACKBEAT

**ELI5:** the big clap spots in many grooves.  
**DRUMMER:** commonly snare emphasis on beats 2 and 4 in 4/4.

## FILL

**ELI5:** a short sentence that says "the next thing is coming."  
**DRUMMER:** temporary variation connecting phrases/sections.  
**USE IT:** let the fill distort subdivision while the landing stays clear.

## BREAK

**ELI5:** everybody gets out of the way for a moment.  
**DRUMMER:** reduced or isolated texture that resets attention.  
**USE IT:** contrast through narrowing.

## HALF-TIME / DOUBLE-TIME

**ELI5:** same road, but your body starts counting the big steps slower or faster.  
**DRUMMER:** backbeat spacing changes the perceived pulse hierarchy while underlying tempo may remain.  
**EINSTEIN:** metrical reinterpretations commonly near 1:2 or 2:1 pulse relationships.  
**USE IT:** create speed changes without literal tempo automation.

# 4. Pitch, melody, and harmony

## FREQUENCY

**ELI5:** how many times something wiggles each second.  
**EINSTEIN:** cycles per second, measured in hertz (Hz). For periodic sounds, fundamental frequency strongly relates to perceived pitch.  
**DON'T CONFUSE:** a song's tuning reference with the idea that "the whole song is one frequency."

## PITCH

**ELI5:** how high or low a sound seems.  
**EINSTEIN:** perceptual attribute related strongly, but not exclusively, to periodic frequency structure.

## NOTE

**ELI5:** a named pitch idea, like `A` or `C#`.  
**EINSTEIN:** symbolic musical category that can represent pitch class plus octave/duration/context.

## INTERVAL

**ELI5:** the distance between two notes.  
**DRUMMER:** like measuring how far one drum is from another, but vertically in pitch.  
**EINSTEIN:** frequency/pitch relationship conventionally labeled by scale-step distance and quality.

## OCTAVE

**ELI5:** the "same" note wearing a higher or lower body.  
**EINSTEIN:** frequency ratio 2:1; A4=440 Hz and A5=880 Hz are one octave apart.

## SCALE

**ELI5:** a chosen ladder of notes.  
**EINSTEIN:** ordered pitch collection used as a tonal framework.

## KEY / TONAL CENTER

**ELI5:** the note/chord that feels most like home.  
**EINSTEIN:** hierarchical tonal organization around a perceived reference pitch/chord; generated music may be ambiguous or modal rather than cleanly keyed.  
**USE IT:** say `tonal candidate` when evidence is uncertain.

## MODE

**ELI5:** same note family, different feeling of where home and the steps live.  
**EINSTEIN:** scale/tonal organization characterized by interval pattern and tonal center; examples include major/minor and church modes.

## CHROMATIC

**ELI5:** borrowing notes outside the main ladder.  
**USE IT:** create pressure without necessarily changing key.

## CHORD

**ELI5:** several notes acting as one harmonic object.  
**EINSTEIN:** simultaneous or functionally grouped pitch collection.

## TRIAD

**ELI5:** a basic three-note chord skeleton.  
**EINSTEIN:** chord commonly built from root, third, and fifth.

## CHORD INVERSION

**ELI5:** same chord family, different note sitting on the bottom.  
**USE IT:** change bass motion while preserving harmonic identity.

## VOICING

**ELI5:** which copies of the chord notes everybody chooses and where they sit.  
**EINSTEIN:** register, spacing, doubling, omission, and ordering of chord tones.

## PROGRESSION

**ELI5:** the route the chords walk through.  
**EINSTEIN:** ordered harmonic sequence whose function depends on tonal/contextual relationships.

## TONIC

**ELI5:** home base.  
**EINSTEIN:** primary tonal stability/reference function.

## DOMINANT

**ELI5:** the chord that often feels like it really wants to go home.  
**EINSTEIN:** dominant-function harmony generates directed tension toward tonic in functional tonal systems.

## CADENCE

**ELI5:** punctuation at the end of a musical sentence.  
**EINSTEIN:** harmonic/melodic closure gesture creating varying degrees of completion.

## CONSONANCE / DISSONANCE

**ELI5:** smooth fit versus rub/tension.  
**EINSTEIN:** perceptual/contextual stability or roughness/tension influenced by spectrum, interval relationships, culture, expectation, and musical syntax.  
**DON'T CONFUSE:** dissonance with "bad."

## MELODY

**ELI5:** the line you can sing back.  
**EINSTEIN:** temporally ordered pitch contour with rhythm and phrasing.

## CONTOUR

**ELI5:** whether the melody goes up, down, arches, jumps, or stays flat.  
**USE IT:** preserve contour while changing exact notes for a safer transformation.

## MOTIF

**ELI5:** a tiny musical idea that keeps coming back wearing different clothes.  
**EINSTEIN:** short recognizable rhythmic/melodic/harmonic cell capable of transformation.

## HOOK

**ELI5:** the part your brain grabs.  
**DRUMMER:** it can be a beat, bass gesture, vocal phrase, timbre, or transition—not only melody.  
**BRAIN/BODY:** repetition, salience, expectation, and memory all contribute; "hook" is a functional role, not one acoustic feature.

## PHRASE

**ELI5:** a musical sentence.  
**EINSTEIN:** perceptually grouped span with internal direction and boundary cues.

## CALL AND RESPONSE

**ELI5:** one musical voice asks, another answers.  
**DRUMMER:** snare fill answers vocal; guitar answers synth; crowd answers singer.  
**USE IT:** one of the cleanest ways to create stage choreography.

## COUNTERPOINT

**ELI5:** two melodies having a conversation without crashing into each other.  
**EINSTEIN:** coordination of independent melodic lines under harmonic/voice-leading constraints.

# 5. Form and arrangement

## FORM

**ELI5:** the map of the whole song.  
**EINSTEIN:** large-scale ordering and recurrence of sections/events.

## SECTION

**ELI5:** one room in the song-house.  
Examples: intro, verse, chorus, bridge, breakdown, outro.

## VERSE

**ELI5:** the part that usually tells more of the story.  
**USE IT:** often lower/restrained signal so the chorus has somewhere to go.

## CHORUS

**ELI5:** the big return the listener learns fastest.  
**USE IT:** increase identity signal, not automatically every instrument.

## BRIDGE

**ELI5:** a temporary road away from the normal loop.  
**USE IT:** departure should make the return meaningful.

## INTRO / OUTRO

**ELI5:** how the song enters / leaves the room.  
**USE IT:** intro can expose the identity checksum before full density; outro can dissolve or restate it.

## ARRANGEMENT

**ELI5:** deciding who plays what, when, and how much.  
**DRUMMER:** the notes may stay the same while the band changes who owns each job.  
**EINSTEIN:** distribution of musical functions across instruments/voices/register/time.

## DENSITY

**ELI5:** how crowded the musical room is.  
**EINSTEIN:** event/voice/activity occupation over time; not the same as loudness.

## NEGATIVE SPACE

**ELI5:** the empty floor that lets you see the dancers.  
**DRUMMER:** rests make the hits hit harder.  
**BRAIN/BODY:** absence creates expectation and segmentation; silence is active information.

## CONTRAST

**ELI5:** making two moments clearly different.  
**USE IT:** contrast can come from less—narrower, drier, sparser, lower, stiller—not just "bigger."

# 6. Sound and synthesis

## TIMBRE

**ELI5:** why a guitar and piano playing the same note still sound different.  
**EINSTEIN:** perceptual sound quality shaped by spectral envelope, harmonics, noise, temporal envelope, modulation, transients, and context.

## OVERTONE / HARMONIC

**ELI5:** extra higher vibrations riding above the main pitch.  
**EINSTEIN:** spectral components above a fundamental; harmonics occur at integer multiples of the fundamental.

## ENVELOPE

**ELI5:** the shape of a sound from birth to death.  
**EINSTEIN:** time-varying amplitude or control contour.

## ATTACK

**ELI5:** how the sound begins.  
Sharp attack = click/punch; slow attack = fade/bloom.

## DECAY

**ELI5:** how the sound falls after the first hit toward its held level.

## SUSTAIN

**ELI5:** how much of the sound stays while you keep holding it.

## RELEASE

**ELI5:** how the sound disappears after you let go.

## TRANSIENT

**ELI5:** the tiny sharp front edge that says "a hit just happened."  
**DRUMMER:** stick crack, kick click, pick attack.  
**EINSTEIN:** short-duration, high-change signal event around onset.

## OSCILLATOR

**ELI5:** a machine that wiggles in a repeating shape to make/control sound.  
**EINSTEIN:** signal source producing periodic waveforms such as sine, saw, square, triangle.

## FILTER

**ELI5:** a door that lets some frequency neighborhoods through more than others.

## LOW-PASS FILTER

**ELI5:** keeps the lows, turns down the highs.  
**USE IT:** narrowing/underwater/behind-the-wall contrast.

## HIGH-PASS FILTER

**ELI5:** keeps the highs, removes/reduces lows.  
**USE IT:** create a weightless pre-return section.

## RESONANCE (FILTER)

**ELI5:** making the edge of the filter ring more loudly.  
**DON'T CONFUSE:** with Schumann resonance; same word, different phenomenon.

## MODULATION

**ELI5:** one thing repeatedly moving another thing.  
Examples: volume wobble, filter movement, vibrato, panning.  
**EINSTEIN:** time-varying control of signal parameter by another signal/function.

## AMPLITUDE MODULATION / TREMOLO

**ELI5:** volume moving up and down repeatedly.  
**EINSTEIN:** modulation of signal amplitude by a periodic or other control waveform.  
**USE IT:** a sub-audible control rate such as ~7.83 Hz can become an audible rhythmic modulation of an audible carrier.

## FREQUENCY MODULATION

**ELI5:** one oscillator bends another oscillator's pitch very quickly.  
**EINSTEIN:** instantaneous frequency varies as a function of a modulating signal, producing sidebands and complex spectra.

# 7. Mix, EQ, dynamics, and space

## EQ / EQUALIZATION

**ELI5:** a volume knob for frequency neighborhoods.  
**DRUMMER:** make kick body clearer without turning up the whole drum kit.  
**EINSTEIN:** frequency-dependent gain shaping using filters.  
**USE IT:** talk about roles—remove masking, create presence, protect bass—rather than "better EQ."

## LOW / MID / HIGH

**ELI5:** bass floor / body-and-voice middle / sparkle-and-edge top.  
**EINSTEIN:** broad spectral regions whose exact boundaries are context-dependent.

## PRESENCE

**ELI5:** the part of the sound that feels closer and easier to understand.  
**EINSTEIN:** commonly associated with upper-mid frequency energy and articulation, but not a single fixed band.

## AIR

**ELI5:** the very top sparkle/breath around a sound.  
**DON'T CONFUSE:** air with harshness.

## MASKING

**ELI5:** one sound stands in front of another so you cannot hear it clearly.  
**EINSTEIN:** reduced audibility of one signal due to another signal with overlapping temporal/spectral energy.

## COMPRESSION

**ELI5:** an automatic hand on the volume knob that turns loud parts down according to rules.  
**EINSTEIN:** dynamic-range processing governed by threshold, ratio, attack, release, knee, and gain.  
**USE IT:** shape punch and consistency; do not equate compression with loudness.

## LIMITER

**ELI5:** a security guard near the ceiling.  
**EINSTEIN:** high-ratio dynamics processor intended to restrict peaks above a threshold/ceiling.

## SATURATION

**ELI5:** gently bending the waveform until it grows extra color.  
**EINSTEIN:** nonlinear processing generating harmonics and level-dependent coloration.

## DISTORTION

**ELI5:** changing the waveform enough that new roughness/harmonics appear.  
**DON'T CONFUSE:** intentional distortion with accidental clipping.

## CLIPPING

**ELI5:** the waveform hits a ceiling and gets its top chopped off.  
**EINSTEIN:** nonlinear truncation when signal exceeds representable/allowed amplitude.

## HEADROOM

**ELI5:** empty ceiling space before the signal hits the roof.  
**USE IT:** preserve room for peaks and later processing.

## RMS

**ELI5:** one way to summarize the signal's average power-like level over time.  
**EINSTEIN:** root-mean-square amplitude; window choice matters.  
**DON'T CONFUSE:** RMS with perceived loudness.

## LUFS

**ELI5:** a loudness ruler designed to behave more like human hearing than plain peak level.  
**EINSTEIN:** loudness units relative to full scale based on standardized perceptual weighting/integration.

## REVERB

**ELI5:** the room answering after the sound.  
**EINSTEIN:** dense reflection field simulating/creating spatial decay and environment cues.

## PRE-DELAY

**ELI5:** the tiny wait before the room starts answering.  
**USE IT:** separate a dry lead from a big reverb field.

## DELAY / ECHO

**ELI5:** a copy of the sound comes back later.  
**EINSTEIN:** time-shifted replication with optional feedback/filter/modulation.

## MONO

**ELI5:** one shared sound picture.  
**USE IT:** powerful narrowing contrast and translation check.

## STEREO

**ELI5:** two ears get related but not identical pictures.  
**EINSTEIN:** two-channel signal allowing interchannel level, timing, phase, and content differences.

## PANNING

**ELI5:** choosing where left-to-right a sound appears.  
**USE IT:** position stable roles; reserve motion for salient events.

## WIDTH

**ELI5:** how spread-out the sound picture feels.  
**EINSTEIN:** perceptual/interchannel property influenced by correlation, level/time differences, phase, ambience, and spectral distribution.

## MID / SIDE

**ELI5:** `mid` is what both speakers share; `side` is what makes left and right different.  
**EINSTEIN:** common transform `M=(L+R)` and `S=(L-R)` with normalization conventions varying.  
**USE IT:** keep low body stable while widening selected upper detail.

## STEREO CORRELATION

**ELI5:** how much left and right are doing the same thing.  
**EINSTEIN:** statistical similarity between channels; lower values often imply more independent lateral content, but interpretation depends on frequency/time.  
**DON'T CONFUSE:** low correlation with automatically "better width."

## PHASE

**ELI5:** where two repeating wiggles are in their cycles relative to each other.  
**EINSTEIN:** angular/time relationship of periodic components; phase relationships affect summation/cancellation and stereo behavior.

## SURROUND

**ELI5:** sounds can appear around you, not only between two speakers.  
**EINSTEIN:** multichannel/object-based reproduction uses more spatial degrees of freedom than stereo.  
**DON'T CONFUSE:** stereo "surround-like" cues with verified multichannel output.

# 8. Perception, neuroscience, and physiology

## ENTRAINMENT

**ELI5:** one repeating thing helps another repeating system fall into step.  
**DRUMMER:** the click starts, and after a moment your hands/body predict where the next click belongs.  
**EINSTEIN:** alignment of behavioral, perceptual, physiological, or neural dynamics with an external rhythm; definitions and evidence differ depending on whether researchers require a self-sustaining endogenous oscillator or accept stimulus-locked synchronization.  
**BRAIN/BODY:** rhythmic sounds can shape perceptual timing and auditory–motor synchronization; large studies support perceptual entrainment while also showing individual variability.  
**USE IT:** build a stable rail, then create controlled deviations so prediction has something to track.  
**DON'T CONFUSE:** neural entrainment with "the brain literally becomes the song frequency."

## NEURAL OSCILLATION

**ELI5:** groups of brain cells showing repeating changes in activity.  
**EINSTEIN:** rhythmic population-level neural activity measured/inferred at different frequencies and scales.  
**DON'T CONFUSE:** frequency-band labels with simple mental-state buttons.

## AUDITORY–MOTOR COUPLING

**ELI5:** hearing rhythm quietly wakes up the movement system.  
**DRUMMER:** you can feel the beat in your hands before you play.  
**EINSTEIN:** functional interaction between auditory timing systems and motor networks including premotor/SMA, basal ganglia, and cerebellar contributions.  
**USE IT:** make the pulse physically legible before decorating it.

## TEMPORAL PREDICTION

**ELI5:** the brain guesses when the next thing is coming.  
**DRUMMER:** that is why a late snare feels late even before you consciously count it.  
**USE IT:** prediction makes syncopation and snap-back meaningful.

## SALIENCE

**ELI5:** the thing that makes your attention go "HEY, THAT."  
**EINSTEIN:** behavioral/neural relevance or attention priority arising from novelty, contrast, intensity, expectation, context, or meaning.  
**USE IT:** one lateral hit can be more salient than constant motion.

## AROUSAL

**ELI5:** how activated/awake the body-mind system is.  
**EINSTEIN:** multidimensional physiological/psychological activation influenced by autonomic, endocrine, attentional, and contextual processes.  
**DON'T CONFUSE:** musical energy with a single measurable arousal number.

## AUTONOMIC NERVOUS SYSTEM

**ELI5:** body control that handles things like heart rate without you manually operating them.  
**EINSTEIN:** sympathetic/parasympathetic and related autonomic regulation of visceral functions.  
**BRAIN/BODY:** music can influence autonomic measures in some contexts, but effects depend on person, music, task, and study design.

## HEART-RATE VARIABILITY (HRV)

**ELI5:** your heart does not tick like a perfect robot; the gaps between beats change.  
**EINSTEIN:** variation in successive cardiac inter-beat intervals analyzed in time/frequency/nonlinear domains; some metrics relate to autonomic regulation.  
**DON'T CONFUSE:** higher/lower HRV with a universal single-value health verdict.

## INTEROCEPTION

**ELI5:** sensing what is happening inside your own body.  
Examples: heartbeat, breathing, tension, warmth.  
**USE IT:** ask what part of the groove is felt in chest, feet, breath, or jaw instead of only naming a genre.

## LATERALIZATION

**ELI5:** left and right sides are not always doing exactly the same job.  
**AUDIO:** lateralization also means perceiving a sound toward one side from interaural cues.  
**USE IT:** bilateral alternation should move attention intentionally, not constantly.

## BILATERAL ALTERNATING AUDITORY STIMULATION

**ELI5:** sound goes left-right-left-right in a planned pattern.  
**EINSTEIN:** alternating lateral auditory presentation used in some experimental and therapeutic contexts; studies report effects on auditory, attention, memory, and emotional-processing networks, but mechanism and therapeutic contribution are not reducible to the alternation alone.  
**USE IT:** as music, treat it as bounded spatial call-and-response.  
**DON'T CONFUSE:** with a claim that a song itself is EMDR treatment.

## EMDR

**ELI5:** a structured psychotherapy that uses memory processing plus guided procedures, often including bilateral stimulation.  
**EINSTEIN:** Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing is a clinical treatment protocol; bilateral stimulation is one component and research continues on mechanisms.  
**MUSIC BOUNDARY:** `EMDR-inspired bilateral audio motion` describes a creative spatial mechanism only; it is not equivalent to EMDR therapy.

## BINAURAL BEAT

**ELI5:** each ear gets a slightly different steady tone and the difference can create a perceived beating effect.  
**EINSTEIN:** dichotic presentation of two nearby frequencies can yield a percept associated with their frequency difference; research on cognitive/anxiety effects is mixed/context-dependent.  
**DON'T CONFUSE:** binaural beats with ordinary left-right panning or bilateral alternating events.

## SCHUMANN RESONANCE

**ELI5:** Earth and the ionosphere form a giant electromagnetic cavity with natural resonance peaks; the lowest is around 7.8 Hz.  
**EINSTEIN:** extremely-low-frequency electromagnetic resonance of the Earth-ionosphere cavity, not an ordinary audible acoustic tone.  
**MUSIC TRANSLATION:** if requested, use ~7.83 Hz as a modulation-rate target on audible sound rather than pretending the physical Earth resonance is inside the WAV.  
**NEURO BOUNDARY:** auditory entrainment studies sometimes find interesting sensitivity in nearby low-frequency temporal ranges, but that does not prove a unique biological Schumann mechanism.

## 432 HZ / A4=432

**ELI5:** tune the orchestra's reference `A` a tiny bit lower than the common 440-Hz reference.  
**EINSTEIN:** A4=432 Hz is about 31.77 cents below A4=440 Hz; it shifts the tuning reference, not every sound to one frequency.  
**EVIDENCE:** small studies report possible anxiety/physiology differences, but evidence is not strong enough for a unique healing-frequency claim.  
**USE IT:** treat it as a deterministic tuning preset and test preference/result separately.

# 9. MusicOS-native relationship terms

## RAIL

**ELI5:** the thing the song rides on so it can get weird without falling off.  
Possible rails: pulse, bass contour, hook rhythm, chord family, motif, dry drum grammar.  
**USE IT:** lock the rail before C3–C5 mutation.

## LOCK

**ELI5:** "do not change this part."  
**EINSTEIN:** explicit project-state invariant until the user unlocks/replaces it.

## ELASTIC

**ELI5:** it can stretch, but it still belongs to the same family.

## REPLACEABLE

**ELI5:** this part can become something else on purpose.

## FORBIDDEN

**ELI5:** this must not appear in the current design.

## UNKNOWN

**ELI5:** we do not know yet, and that is allowed.  
**LAW:** `UNKNOWN != PERMISSION TO INVENT`.

## SIGNAL WEIGHT

**ELI5:** how much one instruction actually determines the song's identity.  
**USE IT:** remove low-weight adjectives before adding more prompt text.

## ANTI-WASH

**ELI5:** stop all the cool details from turning into soup.  
**USE IT:** primary signal → support → texture → optional color.

## SNAP-BACK

**ELI5:** leave home just far enough that coming home feels huge.  
**DRUMMER:** displacement → clear pocket return.

## STRONG SOURCE / SMALL MUTATION

**ELI5:** if the source already knows who it is, whisper the change instead of rewriting its biography.

## STRONG SOURCE / STAGE DIRECTION

**ELI5:** tell the existing song where everybody stands and how they interact.  
**USE IT:** cast + positions + response rules + contrast schedule + embodiment delta.

## ACTIVITY ELASTICITY

**ELI5:** a live remix can get busier, quieter, or stay equally busy; "live" is not one density knob.  
**LAW:** preserve the compositional rail; mutate embodiment/relationships.

## MICROPOCKET

**ELI5:** a tiny temporary groove-world inside the main groove.  
**DRUMMER:** short displaced/tuplet feel that snaps back before becoming a new tempo.

# 10. Unknown-term behavior

The lexicon is intentionally **extensible**. Do not pretend a finite upload can list every musical term ever coined.

For a term not explicitly listed:

1. identify its domain: rhythm, harmony, form, timbre, production, acoustics, perception, neuroscience, hardware/software, historical notation, or MusicOS-native;
2. define it using the Universal Term Compiler;
3. separate standard definition from genre-specific use;
4. state units/ratios when relevant;
5. give one drummer/body analogy;
6. give one practical use;
7. add neurophysiology only when there is a defensible bridge;
8. mark disputed claims as disputed.

```text
COMMON TERM
→ EXPLAIN SIMPLY

RARE TERM
→ EXPLAIN SIMPLY + DEFINE CONTEXT

DISPUTED TERM
→ EXPLAIN CLAIM + EVIDENCE CEILING

USER-INVENTED TERM
→ PRESERVE USER DEFINITION + MAP TO STANDARD NEIGHBORS
```
