## What This Is

The Follin Program is the compression research axis of JPL. Named for Tim Follin — the
composer who made impossible things on impossibly constrained hardware. The program is
dedicated to the proposition that **compression is creative force, not just engineering**.

We are not trying to compress data. We are trying to compress *meaning*.

## The Core Thesis

**Conuity Fidelity:** The true benchmark of JPL compression is not bytes-per-token ratio.
It is: when Raven decompresses a JPL-encoded entity — a MusicOS track, a MonsterOS design,
a spatial experience, a conversation — does it *feel the same* as the in-head original?

Not data fidelity. **Conuity fidelity.**

MusicOS is the proof of concept. The RGB encoding, the physics vocabulary, the sensory
metrics — they compress hours of musical and experiential intent into a structured prompt
that produces the right feeling. The compression preserved the meaning.

## What We Are Testing

### 1. Information Density Per Token

How much meaning can JPL encode per character, compared to:
- JSON (denotative, no grammar)
- YAML (human-readable, no inference)
- Prolog (relational, no sensory layer)
- MusicXML (domain-specific, no cross-domain)
- Brainfuck (minimal grammar, maximum meaning per symbol)

Benchmark: given X tokens, does JPL encode more *meaningful musical/physics/sensory
structure* than any other format?

### 2. Grammar as Compression Primitive

Structural constraints reduce entropy, not just arithmetic coding. JPL's `@INSTANCE`,
`@IDENTITY`, `@DOMAIN` blocks are not organizational overhead — they are *compression*,
because knowing the structural context allows more meaning per symbol.

Test: how much does JPL grammar reduce the entropy of a given entity vs. the same entity
in raw JSON?

### 3. Relationship Encoding

JPL does not just encode data. It encodes *connections between data*. The trap card is
not a field — it is a structural property of the `@INSTANCE` block. That encoding carries
more meaning than any field could.

Test: can JPL encode a relationship graph (MusicOS physics ↔ MonsterOS mechanics) that
preserves cross-domain inference quality?

### 4. Semantic Lossy vs. Lossless

What compresses away at the token level that matters at the meaning level? What is
irreducible meaning vs. noise?

Test: JPL compression of a MusicOS track — what can be removed without losing the feeling?
What is the minimum viable JPL entity that preserves conuity fidelity?

### 5. Hieroglyphic Encoding

Symbols that carry relational meaning, not just denotative meaning. The trap card symbol
(⚡) denotes "activation condition" AND implies "danger, origin, irreversible."

Test: can JPL define a symbol vocabulary where each symbol carries maximum relational meaning
per character?

### 6. Guardian Property Preservation

The trap card must survive compression. A JPL entity that loses its `@INSTANCE` origin block
is not the same entity — it is a parse failure. The compression must preserve the guardian
property.

Test: can any compression algorithm (lossy, arithmetic, semantic) reduce a JPL entity
without removing the origin binding?
